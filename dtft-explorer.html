DTFT Explorer: Spectra of Discrete Signals

The Discrete-Time Fourier Transform turns an aperiodic sequence x[n] into a continuous, 2π-periodic function of the digital frequency ω. Pick a classic signal, turn its knobs, and watch the magnitude and phase respond — then widen the view to see the spectrum repeat.

X(ejω) = Σn x[n]e−jωn

Principal value in (−π, π]. Jumps of 2π are wrap-around, not discontinuities of the underlying phase; jumps of π happen where the magnitude crosses zero.

Two facts, one formula. The DTFT is continuous in ω because x[n] is aperiodic — nothing forces it onto harmonics. It is 2π-periodic because e−jωn cannot tell ω from ω + 2π when n is an integer. Sampling in time is what made the frequency axis periodic.
