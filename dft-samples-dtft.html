DTFT & DFT · lecture 1, part III

The DFT Samples the DTFT

Take N samples, compute their DTFT — a continuous curve — and the N-point DFT is that curve read at ω = 2πk/N. Append zeros and the DFT reads the same curve at more points. More pixels, not more information.

X[k] = X(ejω)|ω = 2πk/M

Signal and padding

N real samples carry all the information. Zero-padding to M = N, 2N, 4N, 8N samples changes only how finely the DTFT is sampled.

Same curve, denser samples. Zero-padding interpolates the DTFT — it makes the plot prettier and lets you locate a peak more precisely, but it cannot separate two tones the N samples could not separate. Try “two close tones”: pad to 8N and the bump stays one bump; raise N and it splits.
