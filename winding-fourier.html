Winding a Signal: The Transform as a Centroid

Multiply a signal by e−jωt and its graph gets wound around the origin of the complex plane. The Fourier transform at that ω is simply the centroid of the wound curve. Sample the signal and the curve becomes a handful of points, the integral a sum — and the winding rate is measured in radians per sample.

Why the peaks? When the winding rate matches a frequency inside the signal, every cycle of the graph lands on the same side of the origin, the mass piles up, and the centroid moves away from zero. At any other rate the cycles are spread evenly around the circle and cancel.
This page follows the picture in 3Blue1Brown’s But what is the Fourier Transform? A visual introduction (2018), where the transform is introduced as the center of mass of a wound-up graph. The sampled mode is the new step for this course: with only N points to wind, the centroid integral becomes a sum — the Discrete Fourier Series at the rates 2πk/N, the DTFT at every rate in between.
