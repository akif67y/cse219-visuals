windWind the samples around the origin

Sample n is placed at radius x[n] and turned by −ω·n: consecutive points are rotated by the same angle ω — the digital frequency. The sum of the points is X(ejω); their centroid is X(ejω)/N. Nothing repeats, so every ω gives its own value.

Slide past ω = π and the picture mirrors: a turn of more than half a circle per sample looks like a smaller turn the other way — which is why the right half of the spectrum holds the negative frequencies, and why the whole thing repeats at 2π. One curve, M samples of it. Sampling in time made the spectrum 2π-periodic; the DFT reads that curve at ω = 2πk/M. Zero-padding reads it more finely but cannot add what the N samples never knew. Bin k sits at f = k·fₛ/M for k ≤ M/2 and at (k − M)·fₛ/M beyond — same numbers, new rulers.
