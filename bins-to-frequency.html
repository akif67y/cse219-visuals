From Bins to Hertz

A DFT bin is just an index k. Relabel the axis step by step — radians per sample, radians per second, hertz — and nothing about the N numbers changes: bins 0 → N/2 still climb from dc to half the sampling rate, bins N/2 → N still hold the folded negative frequencies.

fk = k·fs/N  (k ≤ N/2)  ·  (k − N)·fs/N  (k > N/2)

Read down the rows under any stem: k → ωk = 2πk/N → Ωk = ωk·fs → fk = Ωk/2π. Hover a stem to see its whole chain.

bins 0 → N/2: positive, low → high bins N/2 → N: negative, high → low k = 0 (dc) and k = N/2 (fs/2) where the tones truly sit (±f₀, ±f₁)
Relabeling changes units, not samples. The stems never move when you switch fs: only the Ω and f rows are rescaled. What fs does change is which physical frequency each bin stands for — and therefore where a given tone lands. That is exactly numpy.fft.fftfreq(N, 1/fs).
k — bin index0 … N−1, as the DFT computes it.
ωk — radians per sample2πk/N. Bins past N/2 are ω − 2π: negative.
Ωk — radians per secondωk/Ts = ωk·fs. π per sample is Ω = ωs/2.
fk — hertzΩk/2π = k·fs/N. Bin N/2 is fs/2; the top bins are −fs/N … −Δf.
